# Summary: Ghana Led Operation Blackout, Not FBI

**Source:** MyJoyOnline (article text credits Graphic Online)  
**Date:** 9 October 2026

The Cyber Security Authority (CSA) rejected claims that the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) led Operation Blackout, also known as Operation Cyber Hunt, in Ghana. According to the CSA, Ghanaian authorities planned, coordinated and executed the operation under Ghanaian law, with support from international partners, including the FBI and the United Kingdom’s National Crime Agency.

The CSA emphasized that foreign law enforcement agencies cannot independently exercise police powers or make arrests in Ghana without the involvement and authorization of Ghanaian authorities. It acknowledged the intelligence contributions of international partners but called for public accounts of the operation to accurately recognize Ghana’s leadership.

The operation reportedly uncovered suspected cybercrime centres in parts of the Greater Accra Region. Eight suspects were arrested, while 120 people suspected of being trafficked and exploited for online fraud were rescued. Authorities seized 136 mobile phones, 130 laptops, internet devices, four vehicles and other items.

The CSA said investigations were ongoing and that efforts were being made to repatriate the rescued victims, most of whom were identified as Nigerians. It reaffirmed Ghana’s commitment to combating cybercrime through intelligence-led investigations and international cooperation.

**Main narrative:** The CSA presents Operation Blackout as a Ghana-led operation supported by international partners, challenging the impression that the FBI was in command.

**Sentiment and framing:** The report emphasizes Ghana’s sovereignty, local leadership and the need for accurate recognition of the roles played by participating agencies.
