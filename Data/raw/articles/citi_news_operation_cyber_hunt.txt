# Summary: CSA Rejects FBI’s Account of Ghana’s Operation Cyber Hunt

**Source:** Citi Newsroom  
**Author:** Patricia Boakye  
**Publication date:** October 9, 2026

## Main Summary

Ghana’s Cyber Security Authority (CSA) rejected the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation’s (FBI) account of Operation Blackout, also known as Operation Cyber Hunt, insisting that Ghanaian authorities led and coordinated the operation with support from local and international security agencies.

According to the CSA, the operation was planned and executed within Ghana’s legal and institutional frameworks. Participating Ghanaian institutions included the National Security Council, the Criminal Investigations Department of the Ghana Police Service, the Ghana Immigration Service, and the Office of the Attorney-General. International partners included the United Kingdom’s National Crime Agency, the FBI, Nigeria’s Economic and Financial Crimes Commission, and the Joint Case Team on Cybercrime.

The CSA acknowledged the intelligence contributions of the FBI and the National Crime Agency but emphasized that all participating agencies should receive accurate recognition. It also stated that foreign law-enforcement agencies cannot independently exercise police powers or conduct arrests in Ghana without the authorization and involvement of Ghanaian authorities.

The article reports that the operation uncovered suspected cybercrime centres in the Greater Accra Region. Eight suspects were arrested, and 120 people suspected of being trafficked and exploited for online fraud were rescued. Authorities reportedly seized 136 mobile phones, 130 laptops, internet devices, four vehicles, jewellery, ten Nigerian passports, and other equipment.

The CSA stated that arrangements were underway to repatriate the rescued victims, most of whom were identified as Nigerians, while investigations continued. It called on the FBI to clarify its public account and acknowledge the contributions of Ghanaian institutions and other international partners.

## Main Narrative

The article presents Operation Cyber Hunt as a Ghana-led operation supported by international partners, challenging portrayals that suggest the FBI independently conceived or executed it.

## Sentiment and Framing

The report emphasizes Ghanaian leadership, national sovereignty, lawful cooperation, and recognition of participating agencies. Its tone is formal and explanatory, while the CSA’s position challenges the attribution of operational leadership to the FBI.

## Research Note

This is a summary of the Citi Newsroom report, not the full article. Claims about operational leadership, arrests, rescues, and seized items are attributed to the CSA as reported by Citi Newsroom. The report should be analyzed as a media account of the disagreement, rather than independent proof of every claim.
